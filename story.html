My story

Short and concrete.

I started with vision because it exposes failure quickly. A model can look good on clean data and still break on small shifts. That pushed me toward robustness and deployment style evaluation.

Stanford Medicine

In 2022 I joined Stanford Biomedical Informatics. I worked on confounders in skin lesion tasks and on physiologically plausible augmentation. The work is driven by one question: what shifts are real, and how do we train for them.

Simulation driven augmentation

For lung imaging, we simulate respiratory motion to generate breathing cycle variation. The intent is simple: train on shifts that actually occur.

Building

I also like shipping systems. At Appraise AI, the loop is tight: data ingestion, retrieval, modeling, and a number someone will rely on. That forces clarity about uncertainty.

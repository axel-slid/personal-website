My story

Short version.

I started with computer vision because it is a direct test of whether a system understands anything beyond pixels. The first models I trained looked good on clean data and then failed on small shifts. That pushed me toward robustness.

Stanford Medicine

In 2022 I joined Stanford Biomedical Informatics. The constraints were clear: distribution shift is normal, labels are imperfect, and evaluation has to match the setting.

I worked on bias and confounder mitigation in skin lesion tasks, and on physiologically plausible augmentation from lung motion simulation.

Building

I also like shipping systems. At Appraise AI, the loop is tight: data ingestion, retrieval, modeling, and then a number someone will rely on. That forces clarity about uncertainty and failure modes.

Abstract

The revised method starts from a more specific brain logic: retinotopic sensory sheets preserve spatial layout, predictive feedback fills in expected input, and fast episodic signals are recruited when the incoming pattern violates the slow model.

Predictive Retinotopic Memory (PRM, ours) adds a topographic completion adapter to a ConvNeXt backbone. A cheap predictor runs for every masked image; a mismatch signal selects a fixed-capacity contiguous subbatch for refinement; the completed sheet is then passed to the unchanged ConvNeXt body. The auxiliary reconstruction objective is self-supervised from the unmasked training image.

Across three seeds on public MNIST with 45% held-out row missingness, PRM improves masked ConvNeXt stress accuracy from 91.1% to 93.1% (+2.0 points) with only 1.4% more parameters. A fixed 25% hardware route recovers 0.95 points while running about 21% faster than the full predictive adapter in the measured seed. This is a robustness/efficiency pilot, not a broad ImageNet or neuroscience claim.

Method

01

Predict topographically

Use a local cortical-sheet operator to fill unobserved sensory locations.

\(\hat{x}_t=f_{\mathrm{topo}}(x_t,m_t)\)
02

Measure mismatch

Estimate novelty only where sensory evidence is actually observed.

\(r_t=m_t\odot|x_t-\hat{x}_t|\)
03

Route fixed capacity

Gather a contiguous top-k batch for refinement so sparsity maps to hardware.

\(I_t=\operatorname{TopK}(r_t),\quad |I_t|=\rho B\)

Prediction turns mismatch into a hardware route.

The new integration makes the biological analogy operational and measurable: preserve retinotopy, predict missing evidence, score observed mismatch, and route a fixed-size contiguous batch.

PRM (ours). The fixed-capacity route is the hardware intervention: all samples take the cheap path, while a contiguous top-k subbatch takes the expensive refinement path. The mapping is inspired by predictive and retinotopic computation, not a claim that the network reproduces cortical circuitry.

What the experiments say

POSITIVE · PRM (OURS)

Retinotopic completion improves stress.

Across three seeds, the full predictive adapter improves masked ConvNeXt stress accuracy from 91.1% to 93.1% (+2.0 points) at 45% missing rows.

HARDWARE TRADE-OFF

Fixed routing is safer than vague sparsity.

The fixed 25% route recovers +0.95 points in the measured seed and runs about 21% faster than the full predictive adapter, though still slightly slower than the dense baseline.

NEXT FALSIFIER

Scale beyond digit images.

The claim now needs naturally asynchronous video, audio, or event-camera data plus fused-kernel measurements. MNIST is a controlled public stress test, not SOTA vision.
